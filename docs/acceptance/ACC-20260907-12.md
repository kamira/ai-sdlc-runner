## Evidence

### The guard's one live subject

Across the twelve scanned files, `test_the_work_order_field_count_matches` — removed in CHG-20260907-12
and replaced by `test_a_field_count_in_prose_is_checked_against_the_thing_it_counts` — had exactly
**one** sentence to check — `data.md`'s *"Seventeen fields, listed in `workorder.WORK_ORDER_FIELDS`"*,
which is true. `docs/API.md:58` is the second sentence it ever met, and it fails on it while that
sentence is also true. Neither I nor the first reviewer had this right until the second measured
it.

### Three rounds, and what each changed

**Round one.** One reviewer chose a marker comment; the other refused markers as *"an allowlist
nothing forces anybody to write"* and proposed the referent **inside** the matched phrase.

**Round two.** Put back to the first with the objection it had raised — proximity is a guess — it
withdrew its own proposal rather than defending it:

> *"The zero-width form answers my objection. C becomes unnecessary and, here, weaker: an author
> can omit an optional marker and leave a claim unchecked."*

It also conceded a finding it had reported backwards: `SCHEMAS.md`'s *"6 of 6 tables built"* is
**correct and guarded**; the stale statements are the section heading of the very section that row
belongs to, and `DATABASE.md:192`.

**Round three.** I built the agreed design and it refused nine sentences, most of them records of
refused alternatives and quotations of withdrawn prose — which is this repository's convention for
saying why something was changed. Brought back with the corpus, both refused the clause they had
agreed on and converged, separately, on keying the exceptions by **sentence**.

### What the second reviewer found in my own list

Two of the eight entries I had filed as mentions were **claims**. `settings.py`'s *"**Two of the
three fields do change whether a stop happens**"* is bold, present tense, and counts `FIELDS` — and
`test_settings.COUNTS_SETTINGS` was written for that exact sentence class, escaping it only because
the pattern said `settings` and the sentence says `fields`. The leak it described, verified:

```
grow FIELDS to four -> the two documents naming it fail -> settings.py keeps saying three -> green
```

Widened, the guard now sees four sentences and a planted `FIELDS == 4` makes all four disagree.

### A mistake the harness caught

The first three mutations edited the new assertions and named their own file as the catcher.

```
CAUGHT       a field count may be checked against the wrong thing again
ANCHOR GONE  an unreferenced field count may go unnoticed again
NOT CAUGHT   the page may state a node-field total nothing derives again
```

Disabling an assertion and running the file that contains it can only pass. Pointed at what the
guards read instead — a document's count, a document's referent, the payload a claim describes —
**3 of 3 CAUGHT**.

### What the round leaves behind, named rather than forgotten

The sweep over `SCHEMAS.md` and `DATABASE.md` turned up statements that are wrong today and are
**not** in this change: the conversation-turn kind count, the export-format count, the computed
model fields, the section heading that says three of five tables, the sentence calling
`conversations` and `turns` uncreated, and the route counts. They are one subject — a guard that
proves a true sentence is present and says nothing about a contradicting one in the same file —
and they get their own record.

## Verdict

**Pass.** A number in prose is checked against the thing its sentence names, a sentence that names
nothing is refused or written down with a reason, and the page that documents the public payload is
inside the sweep it had always been outside.
