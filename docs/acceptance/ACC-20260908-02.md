## Why the count was fixed by asserting it rather than by correcting it

The paragraph carried three numbers. Two are right and one was wrong, and the first version of this
record explained that by saying the two right ones are asserted somewhere. **A seat measured it and
that is false.** Only the fifteen is held, by `test_the_inventory_is_fifteen_…`. Nothing asserts the
thirty: the field-count rule in `test_documented_numbers.py` iterates a `DOCS` list that does not
include this file, no `DOCS` file makes a thirty-fields claim, and `test_schemas.py` holds the *set*
of `as_dict` fields against `docs/SCHEMAS.md`, which would not keep a count true.

So: **one held, two unheld, and one of the two unheld is still right.** The nineteen is one instance
of drift, not a demonstration that unheld counts drift — the thirty is the control, and it has not.

The repair is still the assertion, for a smaller reason than the one first given: an unheld number
fails nothing when it goes wrong, and a held one fails in the commit that makes it wrong.
`test_the_snapshot_is_twenty_one_keys` now holds it — in a test of its own, because a seat refused
putting it inside `test_the_inventory_is_fifteen_…`, where a new snapshot key would have failed
under a name about the inventory. It is a **typed** number in an assertion, not a derived one; that
word was refused too. The mutation proves it is not vacuous: adding a key to `snapshot()` turns it
red.

## The failure that repeated, and what it cost here

Four rounds of review on two records, and in every one of them a correction reached the sentence a
seat quoted and left the same claim standing somewhere else. In this record it happened twice in
one revision: the dismissal of item 4 was withdrawn in the change record and left in the acceptance
record's opening table, and the `known` count was corrected on the page and left in two table
cells. A seat found four such sites; a sweep for the retracted phrasings, run after that, found a fifth.

The sweep takes one command and would have found all five before the push. It was not run, in the
round after the same lesson was written down. **The correction is not the retraction of a sentence;
it is the retraction of a claim, and a claim has as many copies as it was useful to write.**

Not registrable as a mutation: prose damage passes every guard this repository has —
`ledger_check`, the doc-number rules and 2350 tests were green through all five of those sites.

And the shell ate two spans of this very paragraph on the way in: a `-c` string in double quotes
runs what is between backticks. Found by reading the file after writing it, which is the same
remedy as the sweep.
