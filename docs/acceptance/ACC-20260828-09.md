## The guard that caught the first draft

`close()` was first written with `at`. That is the turn's **own timestamp**, and `Turn.as_dict`
refuses a body naming an envelope key: *"a body that can rewrite its envelope makes seq, kind and at
unreliable for every reader."*

The closing turn vanished from the record — and the guard said so **loudly on stdout** rather than
dropping it silently, which is the behaviour that made the mistake cost one minute instead of
shipping. It was added by an independent seat reading that single line.

`DECISION` had already settled the name with `at_node`. The collision is pinned by a test now,
because the next person will reach for `at` for the same reason I did.
