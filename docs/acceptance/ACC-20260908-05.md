## The half a mutation cannot stage

Removing the accounting is what cannot be registered: that mutation runs against a tree where the
race is fixed, so no worker dies, `failures` is empty either way, and the test passes — `NOT CAUGHT`,
correctly, because **the accounting only bites when something else is broken**.

**But the something-else can be registered, and a seat found that this record had missed it.**
Moving `attach`'s `store.add` out of `with self._lock` makes workers die in the store's manifest
write path; the seat measured 13 of 13 runs red that way, with the invariant test staying green 3 of
3. **How many die, and of what, varies between runs and machines** — a seat counted 4 or 5 per run,
this machine 3 — so no fixed number is stated here. An earlier draft said *"six"*, which nobody
measured. That is the second entry in the group: the invariant test holds the reader under the
lock, the hammer's accounting holds the writer under it, and each goes red on its own mutation.
`--only manifest-race` is 2 of 2. What that pins is **the lock boundary, on both sides of it** — not
the consequence. *An attachment posted during a walk came back 500* is still pinned by nothing, for
the reason given further down: pinning it means staging the race four tests could not stage
honestly. An earlier draft of this paragraph said the consequence was no longer unpinned, and
contradicted that sentence two screens later.

**What 2 of 2 does not mean**, because a seat refused an earlier claim that it did. The pair covers
the reader being unlocked and the writer being unlocked. It does not establish the **accounting
mechanism** — that a dying worker is reported at all — which is the entry that cannot be staged on a
green tree and was measured by injection instead. Two mutations, two sides of the lock, and a third
property that neither of them tests.

**And the writer entry had to be rewritten to mean what it says.** Its first version moved
`store.add` above `_require_version` and out of its `try` as well as out of the lock. A seat refused
it: *"a stale-version side effect or newly uncaught exception could also make the test red"*, so a
compound mutation's `CAUGHT` cannot be attributed to any one of the things it changed. What ships
moves only the lock boundary — the version check still runs first and under the lock, the write
keeps its own `except AttachmentError` — and the red it produces is the accounting assertion, *"a
worker died of something other than a stale version"*, over a list of dead workers.

**What that red does not do is name this record's race**, and a seat refused the sentence that said
it did. The deaths are manifest-write collisions, and the mix moves: `WinError 5`, a replace onto
an open `manifest.json` handle — which any reader of the store can be holding, the other workers'
own `store.add` (it reads the manifest before it writes, `attachments.py:177`) and
`_refresh_attachments` under the lock included, not only the walk; `WinError 32`, the staging file
in use, which is **writer against writer**, because `manifest.json.writing` is a fixed name;
`Errno 13`; and occasionally `FileNotFoundError` when the other writer has already moved the
staging file. One seat
saw `WinError 32` dominate, this machine saw `WinError 5` dominate.

So the mutation establishes that **the write belongs under the lock**, and does not isolate which
counterparty the unlocked write collides with. The walk's read is what the invariant entry covers,
from the other side, and it covers it by asserting the lock is held rather than by catching a
collision.

What replaces the accounting-removal mutation is the injection measurement in the table above, and
the reason sits at the group in `tools/mutation_check.py` rather than only here, so a reader
meeting `NOT CAUGHT` finds it where they are standing.

## Three hollow tests, written by the operator, on the way to the one that works

Recorded because each was green:

1. A patch anchored on a remembered test name that does not exist. Caught by the script's own
   assertion, at no cost.
2. A fixture hooked on `Store.all`, which `start`, `instruct` and `attach` also call — under the
   lock. It parked on `instruct`'s read, and the test failed on a stale version: red, for the wrong
   reason. The hook is `order_paths`, which only the walk calls.
3. **A barrier that held nothing open.** It parked *after* the read completed, so no handle spanned
   it, and the test **passed with the lock reverted**. A guard that could not fail, inside the
   change that exists because of one. Found by reverting the fix and expecting red — which is why
   that step is not optional.

## Four threaded tests, and why the fifth is not one

**Everything in this section describes code that was removed.** `REACHED`, `HELD`, the barrier and
the writer thread are named here as history; `grep REACHED tests/` finds nothing in the tree that
ships, and a seat had to point out that three sentences elsewhere in these records still spoke of
them in the present tense.

This is worth more than the fix it guards, because every version was green and each hole was a
different shape:

| | what it did | why it guarded nothing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | parked *after* the manifest read completed | no handle was open across the barrier; **the test passed with the lock reverted**. Found by reverting the fix and expecting red |
| 2 | held a real handle, released it after `time.sleep(0.3)` | the window was load-bearing and unsynchronised. A seat refused it; the other measured it — idle it caught the defect 6 of 6, but under sixteen CPU burners the writer took 1.05-2.43s to reach the replace and **one run in five passed green with the lock reverted** |
| 3 | an event set immediately *before* `os.replace` | it proved the wrapper was entered, not that the replace was attempted while the handle was held. The writer could be descheduled after the event, the parent's join could expire without asking whether the thread was alive, the handle would be released, and the writer would then succeed |
| 4 | a completion event in a `finally`, **and a hold with its own flat ten-second timeout** | the parent's worst case is `2 × REACHED` = ten seconds, so the hold could expire at the same moment and **let go by itself** — the replace then succeeds, the completion event fires, and it is green with the lock reverted. The record's claim that *"the parent releases nothing first"* was false while that number was independent of this one |

**And the fifth refusal ended the approach.** A seat traced the schedule that beats version 4 as
well: the parent waits `REACHED` for the writer to appear, does not see it, releases the handle, and
the writer — slower than that bound, which sixteen CPU burners are enough to arrange — then replaces
successfully. *"No finite multiplier fixes this."* Every version had been an attempt to pick a better
constant, and the constant was never the problem: reading a timeout's expiry as *"the lock held the
writer"* is an inference, and the test claimed to have removed exactly that when it replaced the
sleep.

What ships asserts the invariant instead. `Store.order_paths` is patched to record
`runner._lock._is_owned()` on each call the walk makes, and the test asserts they were all true —
no threads, no barrier, no deadline, nothing a scheduler can change. Reverted: `1 failed` at 0.29 /
0.43 / 8.97s, on the message *"read the store without holding the lock"*. As it ships: `1 passed` at
0.28 / 0.36 / 0.37s, and the green side now costs nothing.

The consequence — that the attachment came back 500 — is measured in this record and is not pinned
by anything, because pinning it means staging the race these four could not stage honestly.

**And these four are not the same as the list in `CHG-20260908-05`'s build history**, which a seat
had to point out: that list is what went wrong while writing the change — a wrong test name, a wrong
hook, no handle, a patch that mangled what it was fixing — and this one is how the guard was hollow.
They share one entry, and the build-history list is no longer given a count here, because it grew
by one after this sentence was written.

Version 4 had answered the deadline question with a named constant derived from that 2.43s. The
fifth refusal made the question moot: there is no deadline in what ships, because there is nothing
to wait for.

## Two things this review nearly recorded and did not

**A four-fold slowdown that was the reviewer's own load.** One run of `tests/test_server.py` on this
branch took 202s with one failure, against 55s and green on `9bcffb5`. The obvious reading is that
the lock is expensive. It is not: a clean re-run is **153 passed in 57.52s**, and `--durations`
shows the new test at 5.09s and nothing else moved. The 202s run overlapped mutation runs and a
`git archive` in the same session. The seat's independent measurement agrees — the walk's read is
~2% of what `attach` already holds the lock for.

**An intermittent test read as a regression.** `test_a_connection_that_says_nothing_does_not_hold_a_thread_forever`
was the failure in that run. It passes 5 of 5 alone here, the file is green on a clean run, and it is
already recorded in this queue's hand-off as intermittent under load. What settled it was running
the same file on `9bcffb5` twice — 152 passed both times — rather than assuming either way.

- **The class itself**, as the reading seat framed it: *a bounded wait whose completion is required
  for the test's verdict, but whose completion result is neither asserted nor otherwise acted upon*,
  plus *worker failures must reach the parent test*. Worth a suite rule; not written here, because
  a rule over every ignored wait would ban waits that use a timeout only as pacing.
- **Whether the race reaches Linux.** The mechanism is Windows file-sharing semantics. Not measured
  on Linux, and the fix is platform-independent either way.
- **The collision rate through the real HTTP route**, which is what would settle priority rather
  than existence. Not measured: the route-level deterministic collision the reading seat described —
  status and body, both manifests' hashes, the state snapshot before and after, then after retry and
  restart — is a larger measurement than this change needed.
