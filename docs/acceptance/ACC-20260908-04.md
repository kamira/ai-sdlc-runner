## The refutation

Everything two builds of this record said about *why* a fixture was needed rested on one sentence:
pytest removes old basetemps with `shutil.rmtree` on a plain path. It was never measured. What was
measured was `shutil.rmtree` on a plain path — which does fail — and the failure was attributed to
pytest.

pytest calls `ensure_extended_length_path` first. Measured four consecutive runs of the two
chain-building modules with no fixture: `pytest-0` (84 top-level dirs, 474 entries, 17 past 259
characters, deepest 391) was removed by pytest at run 4, silently and completely.

The stranded directories that started the item are **locked**. `ensure_deletable` returns `False`
while a lock file is younger than the cutoff, and skips the directory without looking at anything
else. All **five** on this machine carry one. Two of them are also deep — `pytest-6927` and
`pytest-7884` reach 375 characters — which a seat caught the first correction denying; a lock is
sufficient to strand a directory whether or not it is deep. The largest, 826 entries at 2026-09-08
23:43, is a background full suite this session started and killed. The other four are not attributed
to anything.

## What this cost, and where the process failed

The item survived a seat that found it, a handoff, an ordering deliberation by **two** seats, two
builds, and four seat reads. It was refuted the first time anybody ran the thing it was about.

Four specific failures, none of them subtle in hindsight — and the count in this line said
*three* until a seat counted the bullets, which is the same defect one paragraph down from where
this record documents it:

- **The operator measured the component and attributed the result to the caller.**
  `shutil.rmtree(plain)` failing is a true fact about `shutil.rmtree`. Nothing followed from it about
  pytest without reading what pytest calls, and reading it took one `inspect.getsource`.
- **Both seats reviewed the fix, and the first two rounds asked them to.** The prompts named the
  fixture, the warning, the numbers — and not the premise. A seat asked to refute a repair will
  refute the repair. The second round's prompt asked one open question ("anything a run refutes that
  a reading would not"), and that is the question that closed the item.
- **The stranded directories were evidence and were read as a symptom.** All of them carry `.lock`,
  and one is timestamped inside this session's own killed run. Listing the directory would have
  raised the alternative before the first line of the fixture was written.

  **And this paragraph's first draft made the same mistake.** It said *three*, from a loop whose
  first line of output had lost its name, and a seat enumerated the directory and found **five** —
  `pytest-6927` and `pytest-6936` as well, both locked, both older than this record. A post-mortem
  that criticises not listing them, written without listing them. The finding is stronger for it:
  five out of five stranded basetemps are locked, which is sufficient on its own; two of the
  five are deep as well, and that is measured rather than excluded.

- **What is checkable, and where.** A seat drew the line for me after I drew it wrong: I had listed
  the installed `_pytest/pathlib.py` and the machine's temp directory as *"checkable here"*, and
  neither is checkable by reading this git tree.

  ```
  tree-checkable          the empty diff over src/, tests/ and tools/
  environment-checkable   _pytest/pathlib.py's prefixing; the five locked basetemps,
                          their entry counts, depths and timestamps
  re-runnable only        the four-run cleanup sequence and its 84 / 474 / 17 / 391
  ```

  The third row is a seat's run and no artifact in this tree preserves it. The second is checkable by
  anyone on a machine with this environment, and was not by the seat that asked for the distinction —
  its sandbox is confined to the worktree, so it declined to certify the enumeration at all rather
  than accept the table. That refusal is the reason the depths in the row above exist.

## What is still open, and belongs elsewhere

- The five locked basetemps on this machine. `rmdir /s /q` removes a directory of that shape —
  measured on a scratch tree, not on these — and removing them is an operator action. Whether depth
  would *also* have stranded the two deep ones is unmeasured, because measuring it means deleting
  them.
- The AST sweep prototyped in the second build — a guard that a chain-building module opts into the
  teardown — is measured and works, and has nothing to guard now. If a real long-path cleanup defect
  ever appears, it is in this record's history.
- **Whether other items in the same handoff rest on unmeasured premises.** Item 8 in that list was
  already found to contradict the shipped comment it was about. This is the second of eleven whose
  description did not survive contact with a measurement, and the ordering deliberation had already
  corrected four more.
