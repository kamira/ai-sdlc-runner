## What the check found the moment it existed

Three of the 65 shipped mutations were ambiguous. Two are `cmd_run`/`cmd_serve` twins, where the
console path would have been mutated and the tested path left working — the run printing `CAUGHT`
about a guarantee nothing exercised.

The third is the one worth recording. `risk/a node may point at a workstream nobody declared` was
**unique when it was written**. CHG-20260827-22 added a second `if name not in workstreams:` for
the interfaces guard and made it ambiguous as a side effect. Nothing said so; the `risk` group went
on printing `13 caught`.

That is why this belongs in the tool. The failure is not a sloppy anchor — it is **a later change
moving the ground under an anchor that was fine**, and no amount of care at writing time prevents
it.

## Reservations (disclosed, not waived)

1. **Uniqueness is not correctness.** A unique anchor can still revert something other than the
   guarantee its `says` line names. Nothing checks that the label describes the edit, and the first
   draft of the importer's first mutation — which rebound a dict to an equal dict — is the standing
   example that this class exists.

2. **The module's larger caveat is untouched.** This answers *"can the tests I wrote fail?"* It
   still never answers *"did I write the test for the case that matters?"*, which is the limit
   CHG-20260823-50 demonstrated with a `frontier` group that read 2/2 while the defect shipped.

3. **Only anchors present in a readable file are checked.** A mutation pointing at a file that no
   longer exists is skipped by the sweep and reports itself as `ANCHOR GONE` at run time, which is
   the right outcome but is a different check than the one asserted here.
