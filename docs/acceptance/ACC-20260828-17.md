## The corroboration that matters

The check was written from the code, and the handshake gate's list was written by something else
entirely. They name the same five changes, and the two the check *declines* to name — CHG-20260823-48
and CHG-20260823-50, both `superseded` — are exactly the two where an acceptance behind an
unfinished status is legitimate.

That agreement is the strongest evidence here. A check that had merely been tuned until this
repository passed would not have reproduced another reader's list it never consulted.

## The first draft required a field, and four existing tests said no

Making a missing `Conclusion` a problem broke four tests whose fixtures predate the field. They were
right to break: that is a **formatting** requirement, and `_target_chg` already argues the case
against adding one —

> inventing a requirement for them would turn a traceability check into a formatting one. What is
> checked is **disagreement**.

Absence now skips. All 107 acceptances here carry the field anyway, so requiring it would have
bought nothing and cost the argument.

## Symmetry was considered and rejected on evidence

The mirror rule — a change marked `Accepted` whose acceptance did not pass — was measured before
being ruled out, not waved away. Two pairs match its shape, and neither is a defect: the
**acceptance** was superseded while the change stayed accepted. Adding the rule would have shipped
two false positives on day one.

## Reservations (disclosed, not waived)

1. **This does not re-judge the five.** It reads what their acceptances concluded and makes their
   changes agree. Whether each acceptance was sound is what those records are for, and a lint has no
   business reopening it.

2. **`AWAITING_DECISION` is a judgement about six words.** `in progress`, `wip` and `pending` are in
   it on the same argument as `proposed` and `under review`, but none of the three has an instance
   here — they are reasoned, not observed, and a change legitimately reopened for more work after
   acceptance would trip on them.

3. **The verdict vocabulary was validated against this repository only.** 107 records, 0
   unrecognised. A project with different house style would meet the unrecognised-verdict problem
   immediately — which is the designed behaviour, but it is a wall the first time.

4. **Closing the five was a documentation edit, reviewed by reading.** Each new status line names
   the acceptance it defers to; none makes a claim the acceptance does not.

5. **Verified by the implementer.** The finding came from two tools disagreeing, not from review —
   which is the part of this worth keeping: the disagreement had been printed at the top of every
   session for weeks, next to a green check, and neither was read against the other.
