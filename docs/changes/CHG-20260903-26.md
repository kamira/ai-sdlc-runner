## This is the twenty-eighth defect, and it has the same shape as the other twenty-seven

Every defect the CHG-20260901-15 review found was one shape: **a declaration that does not reach the
thing it declares about**, usually with the declaration sitting in the file that breaks it. This one
is that shape reproduced *in the record that catalogues them*, in the same session, by me.

And the irony is native to the file it breaks. `_status_line`'s own docstring:

> *"Deliberately narrow. A document that discusses its own status in prose must not thereby change
> it, which is exactly the failure this replaces."*

That narrowness is correct and is **not** what is being changed here. The house rule shipped in
CHG-20260830-08 is that **status is a field, not a paragraph**. I announced the withdrawal in a
paragraph shaped like a field, and the rule did to me exactly what it was written to do. The fix is
to write the field, not to widen the regex.

## Three checks, and the precedent is in the same file

`_target_chg` checks ACC->CHG **and** CHG->ACC. The second direction was added by CHG-20260828-02
under the heading *"the other direction, which nothing walked"*, on the ground that

> *"traceability is the point of keeping both files, and it only holds if it holds both ways."*

`Supersedes:` is a CHG->CHG edge, invented by CHG-20260903-24 and -25 — the first two records in 154
to carry it — and **nothing walks it in either direction**. The same asymmetry, one field later:

| # | what is invisible today | mirrors |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a `Supersedes:` naming a CHG that does not exist | `_target_chg`'s existence check |
| 2 | a superseded record with no `Superseded by:` back-pointer | CHG-20260828-02's second direction |
| 3 | **a superseded record whose Status field still says it is under review** | the live failure above |

Check 3 is the one that would have caught this. Checks 1 and 2 are the ones that keep it caught.

## What is NOT changed, and why

- **`_status_line`'s regex is untouched.** Widening it to accept `## Status: <prose>` would make the
  two headings both match, and the file would then have to decide which wins — reintroducing exactly
  the prose-changes-status failure the narrowness replaced. The record is edited; the parser is not.
- **The `IN_PROGRESS` vocabulary is untouched.** `withdrawn`, `superseded` and `abandoned` are
  already in it (`ledger_check.py:62`) and always were. Nothing was missing from the vocabulary; the
  status simply never reached it.
- **The three checks are prospective**, from `SUPERSESSION_REQUIRED_FROM`, for the reason
  `BRANCH_REQUIRED_FROM` gives in this file: the 154 are history, and a lint that fails on history
  is a lint that gets disabled.

  successor carries a `Related` line back. Check 2's back-pointer field is what makes the trail
  walkable from either end; the records themselves are annotated, not edited.

## Risk & rollback

- **The lint gets stricter on two records that exist today** — -24 and -25 are the only carriers of
  `Supersedes:`, and both name a real record, so checks 1 and 3 pass on them the moment they land.
  Check 2 fails until task 2 ships, which is the point: it is red for exactly as long as the ledger
  is inconsistent.
- **A record may be legitimately superseded and still under review** — no. If it is superseded it is
  not under review; that is what the word means, and `IN_PROGRESS` already holds `superseded` for
  precisely this transition.
- Rollback: revert. The lint returns to green and the ledger returns to lying.
