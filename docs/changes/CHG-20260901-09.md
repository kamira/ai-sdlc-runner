## The gap

```
$ grep -rn "HALT_INDEPENDENT" src/ai_sdlc_runner/*.py
policy.py:50   HALT_INDEPENDENT = "halt_independent"
policy.py:53   STOPPING = (CONFIRM, HALT, HALT_INDEPENDENT)
policy.py:184  "acceptance": {..., "high": HALT_INDEPENDENT}
```

Three lines: the definition, membership of `STOPPING`, and one table entry. `policy.stops(v)` is
`v != AUTO`, so nothing anywhere distinguishes it from `HALT`. README:141 has said so since it was
written.

④ is two claims. *High risk stops for a person* is true — at `high`, six gates resolve to `HALT`,
one to `HALT_INDEPENDENT` and one (`pr`) to `CONFIRM`: **eight stops, seven of them halts**. (The
first draft said "eight gates halt", lifting the README's "Stops at 8" column and relabelling it.)
*The person is independent* is a word in a constant name.

## What the first draft got wrong

It proposed `Approval.by: Optional[str]` — an identity the caller supplies — and refusing a
matching or absent one at `halt_independent`.

**This repository has rejected exactly that, twice, and the reasons are in the source.**
`server.py:123-128`:

> Task 15 asked for a server-issued credential rather than a name in a request body, and gave the
> reason: a submitted name is the button captioned "Accept (as verifier)" moved one layer down and
> called enforcement. Here the identity is what the caller **proved** — it held the token — and the
> label is the OS user the server runs as, which the caller cannot choose.

`docs/defect-log.md` records it at :107 (*"`halt_independent` was enforced by a button caption"*)
and again at :436. The first draft cited none of it and would have been the third iteration of one
logged defect.

Three more findings, all measured, that the first draft got wrong on its own terms:

- **It aimed at the wrong ledger.** `--confirm` produces bare gate-name **strings**, not `Approval`
  objects. Putting `by` on `Approval` leaves `--confirm acceptance` on a high-risk run working
  exactly as today — and "that breakage is the correct breakage" was the record's own headline
  justification. The change would have been a no-op.
- **The console has no identity that can differ.** `Runner.approve` builds `Approval(gate, node_id)`
  and accepts no identity from the request; `Operator.name` is minted once per process from the OS
  user. So the two `by` values would necessarily be equal and `halt_independent` would be
  permanently unopenable — not "may be unusable", structurally so.
- **It is not one gate.** `policy.verdict`'s autonomy tightening promotes any `auto` gate to any
  `STOPPING` value, and a plan carrying `autonomy: "halt_independent"` puts the grade on every gate
  — including `self_verify`, whose comment reads "Never stops", and `before_dispatch` itself, which
  leaves nothing earlier to compare against. The three-grep audit missed this fourth site.
- It also named `RunReport.confirmed`, which does not exist. The trace is
  `RunReport.confirmations`, a list of rendered prose; `RunConfig.confirmed` is the input ledger.

## The design

**Independence is what the caller proved, not what they typed.** That is this repository's existing
rule and this change adopts it rather than arguing with it.

1. **An approval records the identity the server already knows** — the one derived from the token
   presented, not a field in the body. `Approval` gains it; `None` keeps today's meaning for the
   CLI, which has no caller to prove anything.
2. **`halt_independent` requires two distinct proved identities**: the one that opened
   `before_dispatch` and the one opening `acceptance` must not be the same. Which two acts must
   differ is open question 1 and is the operator's to settle.
3. **Therefore it requires more than one token.** The server mints one today. Independence is
   unachievable until a second operator can be issued a credential, and that — not a string field —
   is the actual prerequisite. **This change is that prerequisite or it is nothing.**
4. **The CLI cannot satisfy it, and says so.** `--confirm acceptance` on a high-risk run is refused
   with a message naming why: an untargeted confirmation from a shell proves nothing about who is
   confirming. That is the breakage the first draft claimed and did not have.

## Open questions for the panel and the operator

1. **Which two acts must be independent?** `before_dispatch` vs `acceptance` is the proposal.
   `qa_verify` vs `acceptance` is the tighter reading — running it and accepting it are the two
   closest together. **This is the operator's call**, and the README's phrasing cannot settle it:
   it says the acceptor must differ from *the builder*, and the builder is a **model**, so a person
   always differs and the rule is trivially satisfied.
2. **Is a second credential in scope?** Without it this change is unshippable and with it this is a
   change about authentication, which is a different thing from a change about acceptance.
3. **What happens to a single-operator installation?** A high-risk change becomes unacceptable. That
   may be exactly right and it may get the grade downgraded in practice — and the cheapest downgrade
   is `medium`, where `acceptance` is `auto` **and** `lead_review` and `qa_verify` stop stopping.
   The route-around halves the run's stops.
4. **Keyed on the verdict or on the gate?** On the verdict, `autonomy: halt_independent` demands
   independence at `self_verify`. On the gate name, the grade means nothing anywhere else — which is
   this record's own opening complaint.

## Risk & rollback

- **Nothing here ships without a second credential.** That is the honest shape of ④ and it is why
  this record's first task after review is a question for the operator.
- **`--confirm acceptance` stops working on a high-risk run** — this time for real, since the
  refusal is on the path `--confirm` actually takes.
- **An unauthenticated identity would be worse than none.** Today `report.confirmations` says
  `confirmed by the operator` — a role, claiming nothing. A name field would make it read as a
  verified fact about *who*, checked by a comparison the system cannot make. That is the trap the
  first draft walked into and the reason this draft has a prerequisite instead of a field.
- Rollback: revert. `halt_independent` returns to being a synonym for `halt`, and README:141 goes
  back to being true.
