## The gap

> Line numbers checked against `origin/main` at `7e9c276`. CHG-20260823-51 moved them after this record was written; each is quoted by its own text as well as its line, so the next shift is visible instead of silently pointing somewhere plausible.

`engine.py:377` — `risk: str = "high"`, one scalar per run. `engine.py:1319` — `resolve_verdict(node, cfg.risk, cfg.autonomy)` — resolves **every** gate
from it.

At small scale that is right: one change, one grade. At the shape now being targeted, one
instruction becomes several workstreams — a schema change, a copy tweak and a payment path are not
the same risk, and today the copy tweak either drags the whole run to `high` or the schema change
rides along at `low`.

Both failures are bad, and they fail in opposite directions, which is the sign the scalar is the
wrong shape rather than the wrong value.

## The rule this change has to pick

When a node belongs to a workstream, it reads that workstream's grade. The open question is what
the **change-level** gates read — `pr`, `merge`, `close_out` belong to no workstream.

This change proposes: **the strictest grade among the workstreams**, not an average and not a
declaration. A programme is as risky as its riskiest part at the point where all of it merges.

## Risk & rollback

- A plan with no workstreams behaves exactly as today — one grade, every node. That is the
  compatibility path and it is the default.
- Rollback: revert; plans without workstreams are unaffected either way.

## Status

**Proposed.** Reads better after CHG-20260827-17, which decides how a grade is produced.

### Not claimed

**That workstream boundaries are knowable when the plan is written.** They are a planner's guess.
A workstream that turns out to span a payment path was graded before anyone knew that.

**That the strictest-grade rule is right.** It is the conservative reading. It will stop some
programmes at `merge` because one small stream was graded `high`.
