## The gap, and it is two

The README names one:

> **A seat panel that keeps failing has no bound.** `lead_review` → `review_failed` →
> `next_module` → `lead_review` cycles until `max_steps`. The **module** review is explicitly
> bounded — one fix pass, one re-review, then halt — and the seat panel is not.

Reading the graph to fix it found the same loop one node over, unnamed:

```
qa_accept ─fail→ acceptance_failed → next_module → lead_review ─pass→ qa_verify → qa_accept
```

Both are the assembled change being rejected and sent back round, with nothing counting. A run that
keeps failing acceptance dies on `walk exceeded 200 steps` — a step-cap message, which tells an
operator the runner gave up rather than that **their change was rejected twice**.

The module loop has said the right thing since the beginning:

> `halt_second_fail` — *"two failures on one module is not something retrying fixes"*

## The design

One bound for both, because they are one fact: **the assembled change was rejected.** By the seat
panel or at acceptance, in any combination — twice is twice.

```
lead_review  ─fail→ review_failed     ─┐
qa_accept    ─fail→ acceptance_failed ─┴→ change_retry ─first→ next_module
                                                       └again→ halt_change_rejected  ■
```

Two nodes, not four. A bound per loop would count two budgets, and a change rejected once by the
panel and once at acceptance would spend neither — which is exactly the run that most needs a
person.

**Nobody is asked.** `change_retry` reads the run's own record for how many whole-change reviews
have answered `fail`, the way `module_built` reads what the engineer said it built. Counting what
already happened is not a judgement, and a run that could answer it could keep itself in the loop.

**Halting is not failing.** `halt_change_rejected` is `permanent=True`, alongside
`halt_second_fail` and `halt_unreconciled`: a second rejection of the finished change is a thing to
take to a person, not a thing to retry.

## What this does not bound, and why

`qa_verify` has `rejects_to="next_module"` — a **gate** rejection, where a person declined and sent
it back. That can repeat, and it is not this defect: each lap is somebody deciding, not the flow
cycling on its own. A bound there would cap how many times an operator may say no.

## Risk & rollback

- It changes the flow's topology — the gap said so, which is why it needed its own record.
- The change is strictly a **new halt**: a run that reaches it today dies at the step cap instead.
  Nothing that finished before finishes differently, because nothing that finished had two
  whole-change rejections.
- The risk is the *derived* traversal, not the routing: CHG-20260828-15 added one edge and
  `module_cycle()` silently began returning `merge`, `pr` and `lead_review`. Task 7 exists because
  of that, not as a formality.
- Rollback: point both `_failed` nodes back at `next_module` and delete the two new ones.
