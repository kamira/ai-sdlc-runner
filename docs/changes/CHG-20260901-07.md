## What is already there, and what is not

`intake.py` asks for six closed aspects and stops until they are answered
([intake.py:45](../../src/ai_sdlc_runner/intake.py)). Two of them are this territory:

    ("flow", "the flow — what happens, in what order, and where it can go wrong")
    ("ui",   "the screen — what a person sees and what they can do to it")

So the *content* is already required, and a run already halts without it. Three things are not
there:

1. an **artefact** — the aspects are answered in prose, and nothing renders a mockup or a flowchart;
2. a **confirmation** — nothing puts the mechanism in front of a person to agree to before planning;
3. anything that notices the user **did** supply one.

`grep -rin "mockup\|wireframe" src/` finds two incidental comments and no flow logic.

## Why a node and not a seventh aspect

Adding "a mockup" as a seventh `ASPECT` would make the run stop until one exists, which is half of
what was asked and the wrong half. Intake answers *"what is wrong with this requirement?"* — a
survey, union of problems, **no verdict**, and deliberately no gate: `intake_review`'s own note says
a gate there would ask a person to approve the absence of a problem.

Confirming a mechanism is the opposite shape. It is one question with one answer, it is where a
person can still say no, and it needs a gate. That is a decision node.

## The design

Two nodes, because this file's rule is one node one kind of work, and drawing a thing and agreeing
to it are two — the same reason `pm_plan` and `pm_confirm` are two.

```
intake_review  →  intake_confirm  →  mechanism  →  mechanism_confirm  →  pm_plan
                  (CHG-20260901-10)                    │
                                                       └─ no ─→ mechanism   (redraw)
```

`intake_confirm` is CHG-20260901-10 and lands immediately before this pair. The two records were
written from two operator statements a few minutes apart and are one phase: the requirement's gaps
are confirmed, then the mechanism drawn from it is confirmed. **Together they put two stops in front
of `pm_plan` on a default-grade run**, which is open question 4 of that record and is the cost an
operator will feel on every small change.

| | `mechanism` | `mechanism_confirm` |
|---|---|---|
| kind | `STEP` | `DECISION` |
| role | `pm` | `pm` |
| mode | `SINGLE` | `MODEL_PANEL` |
| gate | — | `mechanism_confirmed`, `gate_when="after"` |
| `answer_decides` | — | `True` |
| `rejects_to` | — | `mechanism` |
| branches | — | `{"yes": "pm_plan", "no": "mechanism"}` |

`SINGLE` on the draft for `pm_plan`'s stated reason: several models would mean several candidate
mockups and no rule for choosing between them. `MODEL_PANEL` on the confirmation because it is a
verdict, which is what every other confirmation in this flow is.

**Already supplied is not a branch.** If the requirement carries a mockup and a flowchart, the
`mechanism` node's work is already done and it produces nothing — the same shape as `next_module`,
where an already-recorded module is simply not the frontier. A branch would be a second way to
express the resume rule this flow already has one of.

### The gate

    "mechanism_confirmed": {"low": AUTO, "medium": CONFIRM, "high": HALT}

Mirrors `plan_confirmed`, for the reason `plan_confirmed` gives: wrong mechanisms are cheap to fix
now and expensive to fix later, and this is earlier still.

**It resolves at `high` in practice, and that is not an accident of the table.** `RunConfig.risk`
defaults to `"high"` ([engine.py:406](../../src/ai_sdlc_runner/engine.py)) and no grade has been
proposed this early — `lead_assess` is five nodes later. So on a run where nobody has said
otherwise, this gate halts for a person, which is what ① asks for. A run that passes `--risk low`
has said otherwise, in writing.

## Open questions for the panel

1. **Who draws it.** `role="pm"` because the PM owns requirement→plan. The alternative is a new
   `designer` role, which means a new entry in `policy.ROLES` and a capability set; the flow has no
   design role today and inventing one to own two nodes may be a name standing in for nothing.
2. **What the artefact *is*.** A mockup and a flowchart are text here — this runner writes files,
   it does not render images. The honest form is probably Mermaid plus a described screen, and the
   probe is that both parse. If it is prose that merely *claims* to be a flowchart, this node is
   decoration and the gate stops a person in front of nothing.
3. **Whether `no` should route to `mechanism` or to `intake`.** Rejecting a mechanism may mean "draw
   it differently" (redraw) or "the requirement itself was wrong" (back to intake). `rejects_to`
   takes one target. `pm_confirm` sends both its `no` branch and its rejection to `pm_plan`, so
   redraw is the precedent — but a mechanism a person refuses twice may be a requirement problem,
   and nothing here bounds it the way `re_review` bounds a fix.
4. **Whether this is one gate or two.** Drawing something nobody asked for costs a model call; the
   gate is on the confirmation only. If a run should be able to stop *before* the draft on a
   high-risk change, that is a second gate and the table grows a row.

## Risk & rollback

- **Every run gains two nodes and one stop.** On a default-grade run that stop is a halt, so a
  pipeline that never passed `--risk` will now wait for a person where it did not. That is the
  point of ①, and it is also the largest behaviour change this repository has made to the flow.
- Rollback: revert. The flow returns to `intake_review → pm_plan`.
