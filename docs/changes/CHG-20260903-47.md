## Read this first

`settings.py` says, and `docs/structure/data.md` repeats:

> **Settings cannot touch a gate verdict, a permanent halt, or the adjudication rule** —
> `policy.py` owns those and takes no input, and `test_settings.py` asserts the absence rather than
> trusting this paragraph.

Measured, both halves fail.

```
arm 1 — the adjudication rule, over a split panel
  review_seats=None, high_risk_mode=False   3 seats   pass
  review_seats=2,    high_risk_mode=True    2 seats   undecided
  review_seats=1,    high_risk_mode=True    1 seat    pass      <- `undecided` unreachable

arm 2 — a permanent halt, same node, same operation
  ordinary_commands=()          STOPS: "names target(s) this runner does not recognise"
  ordinary_commands=("pnpm",)   no stop
```

`undecided` is the outcome that means nobody could settle it and **a person must**. At one seat it
cannot arise at all. Both movers are `settings.FIELDS` entries.

## The file contradicts itself three lines apart

`settings.py`'s own third bullet, added by CHG-20260903-28:

> `ordinary_commands` — **vouches commands, so an undeclared target stops being refused.**

That is arm 2, stated accurately, directly beneath the sentence denying it. CHG-20260903-28
corrected *"lower the seat floor and can do nothing else"* and left *"cannot touch … a permanent
halt"* standing above its own correction — the same shape as `ARCHITECTURE.md` defining `asks` two
lines below misusing it (CHG-20260903-43).

## And the test named for the claim asserts a tuple of strings

```python
def test_settings_cannot_reach_a_gate_verdict_or_a_permanent_halt():
    assert settings_mod.FIELDS == ("review_seats", "high_risk_mode", "ordinary_commands")
    assert set(settings_mod.Settings().as_dict()) == set(settings_mod.FIELDS)
```

Its docstring restates the claim in prose — *"None of the three can reach a gate verdict, a
permanent halt kind, or the adjudication rule"* — and its body checks a field list. **Green on the
defect, red on a rename.** Fourth time this class has been corrected today.

## What is actually true, and what is disclosed instead

Settings cannot change the **definitions**: `policy.GATES`, `policy.PERMANENT_HALT_KINDS` and the
`adjudicate` rule itself take nothing from here, and that is now asserted by measurement.

Two of the three fields **do** change whether a stop happens. Both are named in the paragraph and
pinned by a test each, so the consequence is disclosed rather than denied.
