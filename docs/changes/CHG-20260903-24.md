## Why this is its own record

CHG-20260901-15 went through five drafts. Four seats failed every one; the conformance seat vetoed
five times. In the fifth round the **idiom** and **risk** seats, working in separate worktrees and
without seeing each other's answers, reached the same recommendation:

> **Split the change.**

The accounting they each did independently:

| | falsifies | delivers |
|---|---|---|
| **the pre-answer** (this record) | **zero** governing texts | a stop that arrives with the panel's outcome in front of the person, and an exit code that distinguishes three states that all return `0` today |
| **the standing pre-authorisation** ([-25](CHG-20260903-25.md)) | **eleven** | **one cell**, already reachable on `main` by a signed change class |

Every one of the eleven texts — *"stops and returns"*, *"stop and ask a person"* ×3, *"`confirm`
asks"*, *"the cheapest place a person can still say no"*, *"approval, not attention"*, *"a human
sees it"*, *"never a model's judgement about an instance"*, *"a human can still say no"*, FR-12's
enumeration — is falsified by the **pre-authorisation** and by **none** of them by the pre-answer.
A person still stops, still reads, still presses.

Shipping them together meant the half with zero reach gated the half with real value behind eleven
rewrites, two annotated acceptance records and a new store table. So they are two records now, and
this one can proceed on its own merits.

## The design

### `ANSWERED_FIRST` — one bit, in `policy`; the arrangement stays in `graph`

```python
#: Whether a stop is answered first by a person or by the node's own voices. Data, beside `GATES`.
#: Partial: a gate absent from this table is answered by the operator, and editing the table can
#: never delete that fallback — `DEFAULT_RECIPIENT`'s argument, and `HALT_ROUTING`'s shape.
ANSWERED_FIRST: Dict[str, str] = {}          # gate -> "node"
DEFAULT_ANSWERER = "operator"

def answering(gate: str, table=None) -> Tuple[str, str]:   # (who, source); str in, str out
```

`str` in, `str` out, keyed by the same thing its argument is — `routed_to`'s shape exactly. Three
earlier drafts got this wrong in three different ways, each killed by a seat:

- draft two wrote `models`, a **value** of `policy.adjudicate`'s `voices=` parameter — which at
  `lead_review` would have silently stripped `conformance`'s veto, because that branch is documented
  *"No voice vetoes."*
- draft three wrote `voices`, the **parameter's own name**. An escalation, not a fix.
- draft four returned four answerers from a resolver in `policy.py` taking a `graph.Node` — which
  reverses the only dependency edge this codebase has (`policy.py` imports nothing from the package;
  `graph.py` imports policy *inside a function*), returns `node.mode` verbatim at three of four, and
  collides with `graded`, already spent in `policy.py` as `relax`'s first parameter.

**Which arrangement answers** is read in `engine`, which already imports both modules and already
selects on it — `if node.grades_risk: adjudicate_grade(...) else: adjudicate(..., voices="models")`.
No new vocabulary, and nothing renames a mode.

### The pre-answer is the run's own adjudication record

`walk` already writes exactly this, at four sites:

```python
report.adjudications.append({"node_id": node.id, **outcome, "verdicts": dict(verdicts)})
```

**The idiom is to spread the adjudicator's own output**, not to enumerate a shared subset. Draft
four enumerated five keys — which at `lead_assess` carried `vetoed`, a key `adjudicate_grade` never
returns, and **dropped `grade` and `proposed`**, the only keys carrying what that panel decided.
`CHG-20260901-18` is titled *"the grade a panel decides reached nothing that names it"*; draft four
reproduced it in the design that cites it.

So the pre-answer is a **reference to the run's `adjudications` entry for that node**, spread the
same way — and the render is per-answerer: `.voices` for the two binary panels, `grade` with
`proposed` for the graded one, three-valued in `policy.RISKS` order.

### Seven gates gain zero dispatches; two need a new ask

```
pre-answer already in hand at gate time  [======]  6   gate_when="after", excluding lead_review
lead_review — after, but not free        [=]      1   `_adjudicate` runs after `_gate`
needs a new ask and an order shape       [==]     2   pr, merge — gate_when="before"
never stops at any grade                 [==]     2   self_verify, task_review
```

`merge` does not fail on adjudication — a `SINGLE` node can recommend perfectly well. It fails on
**phase**: the gate fires before the node runs. Draft three said "nine gates gain a dispatch" and,
sixty lines later, that seven could reuse the node's answer; both cannot be true.

### On a default install there is no pre-answer at any gate that stops

Measured, `node_models = {}`: `walk` enters the panel path only on
`node.mode == MODEL_PANEL and len(configured) > 1`, so the five model panels take the single-ask path
and produce no `outcome`, `reason` or `verdicts`. The three `single` gates never adjudicate by
design. `lead_review` adjudicates always — but after its own gate.

**This is a prerequisite task, not an open question.** `CHG-20260901-18` D-2 ruled on this shape:
its defect was code living inside that same guard, and its fix rule was *"Keyed on the answer, never
on the mode."* Where no adjudication exists the gate says so, rather than rendering one voice inside
a panel-shaped block.

## The one open question

`lead_review` is the only `after` gate whose pre-answer is not free. `_adjudicate` runs *after*
`_gate("after")` and calls `_send_back`, which mutates the report — so pre-answering it means moving
adjudication ahead of the gate.

And measured by the defect seat, that is **not a reordering**: today the stop is byte-identical
whether the panel unanimously passed or unanimously failed. Putting the outcome in front of the
person changes what approving `lead_review` means. It is the one gate whose veto the whole
derivation exists to protect.

## Risk & rollback

- **Two gates gain a dispatch**, `pr` and `merge`. The other seven `after` gates gain none.
- **A pre-answer is a recommendation a person may follow without reading.** The stop survives; the
  quality of attention behind it may not. Task 13 makes the *absence* of reading visible —
  divergence against `conversations.decision`, latency against every turn's `at`. Nothing makes the
  presence provable.
- **`lead_review` is the sharp one** and it is the open question, not a task with a known cost.
- **This falsifies no governing text.** That is the reason it is a separate record, and it is the
  claim a seat should try hardest to break.
- Rollback: revert. Every gate answers cold.
