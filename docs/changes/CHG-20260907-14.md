### The quotations were stale in their words, not only in their count

```
grep -rn "both are computed" src/     ->  nothing
models.py:419                         ->  "Storing one would let a stale label outlive the truth."
```

Three documents quoted a comment that stopped existing when the exclusion moved into `COMPUTED`.
Singular *one*, not *both* — and no rule looked, because a fence is not prose and the italic form
is what `_prose` strips as a **withdrawn** sentence. The same marker means two opposite things to
two rules, and only the **attribution** tells them apart.

## The guard that existed and was never pointed at it

`tests/test_schemas.py` has `test_every_name_the_entry_lists_is_a_real_name(entry, names)`,
parametrised with the tuple each documented section enumerates. It had six entries.
`models.COMPUTED` was not one of them, and neither was the model-registry section — so the guard
written for exactly this class watched six sets and not the seventh. Two more sites had half the
derivation: `test_models_schema.py` asserted the **persisted** side of
`as_dict() - COMPUTED` and never the computed side; `test_api_schema.py` looked for the string
*"never persisted"*.

## An assertion that never proved anything

```python
assert "reach" not in inspect.getsource(models.save).split("if k not in")[1].split(")")[0] \
    or "reach" in inspect.getsource(models.save)          # stripped on save
```

Measured: clause A **True**, clause B **False**, so it passes on A — whose literal meaning is
*"reach is not in the strip list"*, the opposite of the comment. Its `.split(")")[0]` returns 150
characters of unrelated source, because the list is now the identifier `COMPUTED`.

A reviewer traced it to `599ab8d` (CHG-20260823-22). At that commit `save` excluded a literal
tuple, so A was **false** and B was **true** — and B's meaning is *"the word `reach` appears
somewhere in `save`"*. **It never verified stripping, from birth.** All that changed later is which
branch held it up, and both reviewers accepted that framing: not a guard that decayed, one that was
never load-bearing.

**And a trap in the obvious evidence.** Removing `if k not in COMPUTED` makes `split("if k not
in")[1]` raise `IndexError`, so a mutation over it reports `CAUGHT` — by an **exception**, not by
an assertion failing. Reverting the fix to test the test is fooled here, which is why the repair is
deletion rather than a mutation entry.

## What ships

1. **Seven statements and one docstring** brought to three fields, with the quotations repaired in
   their words and attributed to where the sentence actually lives — `models.COMPUTED`'s own line,
   not `save()`'s body. The attribution rule below caught my first repair getting that wrong.
2. **`models.COMPUTED` joins the name guard** (`test_schemas.py`), and both other sites assert the
   computed half of the derivation they already had.
3. **Prose is checked by subset, a sketch by equality.** A reviewer measured the cost of equality
   on prose: section 10 names eight backticked identifiers that are not fields —
   `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `_model_from`, `claude`, the reach words — so equality there would turn a
   **correct** page red. A fenced key list is different: it claims to *be* the payload, and
   `docs/API.md`'s listed ten keys against eleven.
4. **`test_api_schema.py:238-239` deleted**, and the line above it strengthened to
   `set(models.COMPUTED) <= set(model.as_dict())`. No third persistence test: two already exist and
   CHG-20260903-39 argues by name against another copy of one fact. One reviewer proposed writing
   one and withdrew it on that evidence.
5. **`test_models_schema.py:61` kept.** The other reviewer wanted it deleted as an
   implementation-spelling pin and withdrew that too: it pins the **dependency** on `COMPUTED`, and
   the behavioural tests would still pass if `save` reverted to a duplicated literal.
6. **The duplicated-set-literal rule reaches the documents.** It was scoped to `src/` and `tests/`,
   and `docs/DATABASE.md` carried a sixth copy. The ledger is excluded, because a record of what
   the code used to say is not a copy of what it says.
7. **An attributed quotation is checked against its source**, flattened so rewrapping does not fail
   it — and with comment markers removed before the comparison, because `models.py` carries the
   quoted sentence across two `#:` lines and a plain flatten leaves `#:` inside it.
8. **`store.py` joins the sweep**, and the field rule reads `_prose` like the other two.

## Three things this change found in itself

- **The set-literal rule flagged my own explanation.** My comment for (6) reproduced the literal it
  forbids. Its docstring records the first draft doing the same, and the answer then was to change
  the prose rather than the scope. Same answer.
- **The attribution rule refused my own repair.** I wrote *"`save()` strips them, with the reason
  on the line that does it"* — and the reason is not on that line, it is on `COMPUTED`'s
  declaration. The documents say so now.
- **The field rule was still reading fences.** It was written one change before `_prose` was, and a
  repair to `docs/API.md`'s payload sketch made it report a field count inside a JSON block. A
  helper that exists for exactly this and is used by two rules out of three is a third way to be
  wrong.
