## What was wrong

The page rendered only the turns up to the clock, and the clock starts at zero. Opening
`examples/demo/recording.html` showed **one** turn — `runner opened #0` — while 305 others sat in
the payload, invisible until somebody pressed play and waited.

Measured before the fix:

```
onLoad_turnsVisible: 1
```

Measured after:

```
onLoad_turnsVisible: 306   (runner 153 · model 150 · operator 3)
```

The data was never missing. A reader seeing three operator messages and no responses concluded the
responses had not been recorded, which is a fair reading of a page showing one line.

**A recording has to be a readable log first and a replay second.** The whole log is now built once
at load; the clock moves a highlight through it and dims what is ahead rather than hiding it.
Clicking a turn moves the clock to it, so reading and replaying are one surface instead of two.

## What made it worse

Even once visible, most answers said the bare word **"answered"**.

`_summary` looked for `verdict`, `module`, `modules`, `missing` and fell through when they were
empty — and `{"missing": [], "problems": [], "unsafe": []}` is the commonest review answer there is.
It means *nothing wrong*. A log of 150 lines reading "answered" tells a reader nothing.

Now, against the demo fixture's 150 answers:

```
  92  verdict: pass
  39  note: every module already matches the brief
   6  nothing missing, problems, unsafe
   4  verdict: yes
   2  modules: ["markup", "styles", "weather", "app"]
   1  module: markup
```

An empty list is an answer, not a gap, and it now reads as one. A `verdict` carries its `why` where
there is one.

The `opened` turn had no `text` and no `why`, so it rendered as a **blank line** — the first card
anybody sees. `test_no_turn_in_a_real_run_renders_a_blank_summary` now walks every kind.

## Risk & rollback

- `--format playback` renders differently. Nothing stored changes, and every other format is
  untouched.
- 306 cards built at load rather than incrementally. At this size it is imperceptible; a
  conversation of many thousands of turns would want windowing back, and would then need a way to
  reach the whole log that is not the play button.
- Rollback: revert.

### Not claimed

**That the card count is asserted by the suite.** The cards are built by the page's own script, so
they are not in the served markup and pytest has no engine to run it. The DOM count was verified in
a browser; the suite checks the two halves that were actually wrong — every turn reaches the payload,
and the builder walks the whole array rather than a clock-bounded slice. That gap is stated rather
than papered over.

**That every answer line is informative.** They say what the answerer said. An agent that answers
`{"verdict": "pass"}` to everything produces 150 lines reading `verdict: pass`, and no rendering
fixes that — the example's stand-in agent is exactly such an agent.
