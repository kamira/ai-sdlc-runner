- Proposed by: the operator — 「設計上有安全機制整理確認並提出影響範圍」
- Risk: **medium** — what a stop hands a person
- Autonomy: high

## What the first draft got wrong

It proposed giving `lead_assess`'s answer a new `scope` field — `paths`, `interfaces`, `reversible`,
`blast` — checked for existence and non-emptiness.

**That declaration already exists and is already enforced.** `cfg.operations[node_id]` is
`{"description", "kind", "targets"}`; `policy.derive` reads the targets against
`PERMANENT_HALT_KINDS`, `policy.unrecognised` refuses targets the runner cannot place, and
`engine._permanent_halt` refuses a working node that declares nothing. `undeclared="allow"` is
itself recorded as a relaxation. The first draft never mentioned `operations`, `targets`, or any of
it.

So it would have added a **second, unchecked** statement of what a change touches, and put *that*
one on the halt screen next to the checked one, with nothing reconciling them. Three more findings
against it, all measured:

- `{"paths": ["."], "interfaces": ["none"], "reversible": "yes", "blast": "n/a"}` passes every
  proposed check. Three literal strings — a form nobody can fail.
- Requiring `paths` to resolve **refuses every change that creates a file**, which is the ordinary
  case, not a signal.
- `lead_assess` is a `MODEL_PANEL`. Nothing combines a `scope` across N voices, and reading one is
  explicitly forbidden by the engine: *"reading one of several voices would silently make a panel
  into whichever model happened to be asked first."*
- A refusal keyed on the answer can never be cleared on a resumed run: `_ask` replays the stored
  answer without opening a session, so the run is stuck permanently rather than failing once.
- `scope` is already three things here — `plan_scope`, `sub_plan`'s label, `policy.WRITE_SCOPES` —
  and a fourth in `WORK_ORDER_FIELDS`, on this very node, going the other way.

## What is actually missing

Not the declaration. **The person never sees it.**

`grep -rin "impact" src/` returns nothing at all. Nowhere in a gate stop, a halt reason or the
console does the operator being asked to approve a change get told what it can reach. They are
handed a **grade** — one word — and the declaration that was checked stays in `cfg.operations`,
unread by anything a person looks at.

That is 「提出影響範圍」, and it is one surfacing, not one new field.

## The design

1. **Surface `operations`, not a copy of it.** At any gate that stops a person, the stop carries the
   declared operations of the work it is standing in front of: the description, the kind, and the
   targets — the same data `policy.derive` and `unrecognised` already ruled on, with what they
   ruled.
2. **`feasibility_confirmed` first.** It sits at `lead_assess`, is graded identically to
   `before_dispatch`, and is `gate_when="after"` for the stated reason that the thing a person is
   asked to confirm *is* the assessment. It fires one node earlier with the material in hand. The
   first draft named only `before_dispatch`.
3. **Say what was checked and what was not.** `unrecognised` targets are already known; a target the
   runner could not place is exactly what a person should see, and today it is folded into a verdict.
4. **`undeclared="allow"` is visible at the stop.** It is recorded as a relaxation and it means the
   run is proceeding on a declaration nobody made. A person approving under it should be told.

Nothing new is asked of a model, so no answer contract changes, no plan is migrated, and the
resumed-run stuck state does not arise.

## Open questions for the panel

1. **Does a declaration of what a node touches add up to "the impact of the change"?** It is
   per-node and per-operation; 「影響範圍」 sounds whole-change. Aggregating across nodes is possible
   and is a second thing, not this one.
2. **Is `unrecognised` the right honesty signal, or does surfacing it read as a warning about
   something that is usually fine?** It fires on any target the runner cannot place, including
   ordinary new paths.
3. **How much belongs on a stop before it stops being read?** A halt reason is a sentence today. A
   person who is shown forty targets will read none of them.
4. **Should `qa_verify` and `acceptance` carry it too?** The declaration is what QA should be
   testing against. Carrying it is nearly free; asserting QA used it is not, and this change does
   not assert it.
5. **Nothing confronts the declaration with reality.** A node that declares
   `targets: ["policy.py"]` and edits three files is not caught by this or by anything else. That is
   the honest limit of ② as designed and it should be stated rather than implied.

## Risk & rollback

- **No answer contract changes**, so no plan is migrated and no resumed run is stuck. This is the
  whole reason the second draft is smaller than the first.
- **A stop gets longer.** See open question 3 — a stop nobody reads is worse than a short one.
- **It may read as more assurance than it is.** The operator is shown a *declaration*, checked for
  the kinds it names and for targets the runner cannot place, and not checked against what the node
  actually did. Saying so at the stop is part of the change, not a footnote to it.
- Rollback: revert. The grade travels alone again, and the declaration stays where it was.
